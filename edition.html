Trucking's sticker price just tied its all-time record, with less freight on the road.

November 16, 20266 min read

RatesCapacityReeferContracts

Contract van rates hit $3.28/mi in October, matching the May 2022 record, while freight volume actually fell. Reefer trucks are turning down one in five loads heading into Thanksgiving, and the national fleet is the smallest it's been since early 2022. This isn't a demand story. It's a scarcity story. And it just became your 2027 budget.

$3.28 and nobody ordered more freight: why the record matters more than the number.

Quick refresher, because this is the whole story. Trucking has two prices. The spot rate is the cash price: what a truck costs today, this load, this lane. The contract rate is the yearly deal you lock in with your carriers. Spot bounces around weekly. Contract is the slow, heavy line that becomes your budget. And DAT's October print, released November 10, showed the contract line rising 4 cents to $3.28 per mile, tying the all-time record set in May 2022.

Here's the part that should make you put the coffee down. In May 2022, that record made sense: everyone was shipping everything, everywhere, at once. This time? Van freight volume is down 2% year over year. Reefer is down 9. Flatbed is down 7. Prices hit a record while the amount of stuff moving went down. That only happens for one reason: the trucks are disappearing faster than the freight is. The CDL rule from March carved a hole in the driver pool, and the earliest real relief is still Q1 2027. Fewer trucks chasing less freight, and the trucks are winning.

Why the urgency: it's bid season. Right now, carriers across the country are quoting your 2027 rates, and every one of them just got handed a record-tying number to point at. If you're waiting for the soft weekly spot market to drag contract prices down before you sign, understand what you're actually betting on: that demand weakness will break a price floor that demand weakness just proved it can't break. The record printed on falling volume. That was the test. The floor held.

May 2022's record took a demand boom. This one took nothing but a shrinking fleet. That's your entire 2027 budget in one sentence.

Our read. When a price record prints on shrinking volume, the floor is built out of missing trucks, and no amount of soft demand can knock it down. Carriers will anchor every 2027 quote to $3.28. Don't haggle over the number; change the deal. Rates that float with the market, shorter terms, room to flex your volumes. In a rising market, the shippers who win aren't the ones who found a cheaper price. They're the ones who refused to lock in an expensive one.

Turkey season is here, and reefer carriers are saying no to one load in five.

Thanksgiving is the Super Bowl of refrigerated trucking, and kickoff just happened. Reefer spot rates jumped 10 cents to $3.70/mi the week of November 9 as holiday food freight hit the road. The number to watch, though, is the rejection rate: how often a carrier looks at a load it already agreed to haul under contract and says "no thanks, I can get paid more elsewhere." Reefer and flatbed rejections are both running 20–21%. One in five. California, where a huge share of holiday food originates, is leading the charge: reefer up 15 cents, dry van up 15 cents, the biggest regional jumps on the board. If you ship anything cold or grocery-adjacent: tender this week, give carriers 48–72 extra hours of notice, and line up a backup before you need one. Pay the premium once, not three times after two loads get handed back.

The fleet keeps shrinking, just as the import wave that fed it rolls out.

The supply side isn't stabilizing; it's still bleeding. The active truck count is now the lowest since early 2022. Over 1,000 logistics jobs were cut from late October into November, and two more carriers went down in two days: Bruin filed for liquidation November 9, X-Total filed for bankruptcy protection November 10. Meanwhile, the import surge that kept everyone busy is ending on schedule. November container volumes taper to a forecast 2.05M TEU, closing out a record 26M TEU year. Put the two lines together: freight is stepping down, but trucks are stepping down faster. That's how a record rate prints in a cooling market. One practical order of business before you award a single 2027 lane: check your carriers' financial health. The cheapest bid from a carrier that folds in February is the most expensive rate you'll buy all year.

What to do this week.

Three to do now, two to watch.

  1. 1Do now

    Negotiate the deal structure, not the sticker price

    Carriers will quote 2027 against the fresh $3.28 record. Counter with mechanics: rates tied to a market index, 6-month terms instead of 12, and pricing tiers that flex with your volume. A fixed rate signed against a record print is a ceiling you pay for all year.

  2. 2Do now

    Tender Thanksgiving food freight this week

    With reefer carriers rejecting one in five contracted loads, late tenders get cherry-picked. Submit now, add 48–72 hours of lead time, and pre-clear a backup carrier before the holiday squeeze, not during it.

  3. 3Do now

    Credit-check every carrier in your 2027 bid

    Two bankruptcies in two days and the smallest fleet since early 2022. Pull financial and authority data on every incumbent before awarding lanes. A dying carrier's cheap bid is a February service failure with your name on it.

  4. 4Watch

    Watch December's DAT print for a break above $3.28

    A tie is a baseline; a break is a trend. If November contract rates print above $3.28, re-run your 2027 budget at +5% and +8% before January awards go out.

  5. 5Watch

    Don't let falling imports fool your truckload budget

    November's import taper will ease port congestion and may soften weekly spot rates. It will not touch the contract floor: this record printed while volume was already falling. Budget to supply, not demand.
